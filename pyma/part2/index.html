Part Two

The rest of the thinking

In the room we built your plan. That was page one. This is the part there was no time for: where you actually stand today, what your next six years should be spent on, and what your days will be made of once you get there.

Twelve minutes. Three answers you can only give yourself.
One

The three circles

A career is three circles. What you know, how you grow, and what you show. Almost everyone is strong in two and empty in one, and almost nobody has noticed which.

Answer honestly. Nobody sees this but you.

What I know3
How clearly can you say, in one sentence, what you are good at and where you are going?
How I grow3
How deliberately are you getting better right now? Not learning in general. Better at one thing, on purpose, this week.
What I show3
If a stranger searched for you today, how much finished work of yours could they actually see?

What this says
The world can only see your work. Not your potential. Marks lie. Not the thing you are about to start, only the thing you finished. Whichever circle is smallest is the only one worth working on this quarter.
Two

What your 18 to 24 is for

There are only three currencies a career trades on. A skill people can verify by looking. A credential that opens a door without a conversation. Or experience, the situations you have actually lived through.

Most people are on the credential pathway by default, not by choice. Choosing it is fine. Drifting into it is not.

The blanks are the point. Six years is not long. Every hour on a row marked not the lever is an hour stolen from a row that is. Not the lever does not mean useless. It means it will not move your outcome. Nobody is telling you to drop anything. This is telling you where the compounding actually happens.
Three

What your days will be made of

Every job on earth is a mix of five things. Decide, make, meet, support, monitor. Primary colours. Two people with the same job title can have completely different days, because the title is not the job. The mix is the job.

Put ten points where you want your day to go. Not where you are good. Where you want to spend Tuesday.

Four

Hard skills, soft skills, body of work

Hard skills get you the interview. They are teachable, testable, and they go out of date. Soft skills get you kept, and nobody can verify them from the outside, which is why everyone claims them. A body of work is the only one of the three that argues on your behalf while you are asleep.

Most students spend six years on the first, assume the second, and never start the third. The third is the one that takes the longest to build and the one that compounds. It is also the only one you can start today, badly, for free.

Picasso made about fifty thousand works. The famous ten sit on top of the other forty-nine thousand nine hundred and ninety. Quality is born from quantity. Adi's own body of work began the same way: 150 caricatures, drawn one at a time.

Now go back to page two

The plan you printed has a page you write on. Nothing on this page matters unless a box gets ticked on that one. Start today, badly. Day one is the only day you get to start.

The real work is not out there. It is in here. Start with yourself. Build your body of work.
