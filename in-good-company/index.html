How we opened · Aditya

Before the techniques, one frame: AI is a strategic decision, not a tech problem. The question in this room was never "which tool" but "where does this actually earn its place in the business I'm building." We came from very different industries on purpose, manufacturing to pharma to FMCG, because the cross-pollination is half the value. None of us has it figured out. That's the point of being in good company.

AI is not one thing you "adopt." It is a toolbox. The job of a leader is to know where to use what, pick your core processes, design on paper first, and ship a real prototype in six weeks, not a six-month science project.
The map

Know where to use what

AI is a superset of techniques, not a single tool. Match the question to the technique.

Descriptive dashboards, KPIs · statistics Diagnostic "why is this down?" · statistics Predictive forecasts · classical ML Prescriptive recommendations · ML Computer vision share-of-shelf, invoices · deep learning Generative AI create new, without your data Agentic AI stitches it all into a workflow

"You don't need a gun to kill a mosquito." Some things are still best solved in Excel.

Ten things worth taking back

The playbook

01

Go bold with the core

Not the periphery. Take five core processes, choose two for the year. Reimagine finance, manufacturing or sales, not an investor-report generator.

02

Don't throw away what you have

Old bots, dashboards, legacy systems are pillars you stitch together. Build agents around SAP; don't rebuild SAP.

03

Prototype in six weeks

On your own real data, fully integrated, even for one small region. A toy in a box that isn't integrated will not work.

04

Design on paper first

If you can't draw the process and mark where humans approve and agents act, you can't build it. No paper design, no go.

05

Choose on AI economics

Run a 2x2 where one axis is whether it makes economic sense. Direct value, indirect value and cost decide the final two.

06

Tame token cost with a router

Simple questions answer from existing dashboards (cheap); only real reasoning hits an LLM. One bank let everyone "just query" and killed it in three months on cost.

07

Format and silos no longer matter

Dump Excel, PDF, even legacy design IP as-is. Agents will read it, but only if you give them the knowledge base. They are not magic.

08

Humans and agents, one screen

Build the human-in-the-loop in, with confidence scores and tolerances ("below 98%, notify a human"). No parallel human system.

09

Visibility is non-negotiable

Show every step: what was extracted, what's pending, where exceptions are. Done right, it's more transparent than a human team.

10

Go modular

Break a big program into 8-week sprints that each stand alone but add to one number. Britannia's field-only move drove ~4% incremental top line.

On people & judgement

The human edge

·

LLMs only where reasoning lives

Front-line, deterministic tasks don't need them. Reserve the expensive thinking for genuine strategy questions.

·

AI flatters you

By the 11th iteration it often just mirrors your own thinking. Original thinkers pull ahead; pure tool-users plateau.

·

Awareness at the manager level

Ask people to whiteboard a paper design first. You can tell genuine thinking from copy-paste.

·

Integration is simpler than you fear

Agents talk to systems through plain APIs (and MCP where it exists). No MCP? A simple file drop-off works.
