# In Good Company · Edition 01 — Opening brief

**Slot:** 5:00 PM, Opening & welcome (~90 seconds). Hands off to Moumita at 5:10.
**Frame to land:** AI is a strategic decision you own, not a tech problem you delegate.
**Anchor story:** The STD booth (from Adi's own essay, *Automation: will my job still exist 5 years from now*).
**Close of the evening (6:00 slot):** owner vs operator. *Pocket story for Q&A:* the Sarpanch and the doubled wages.

---

## The opening (read-aloud, ~90 seconds)

Thank you for being here. I know what it takes for a room like this to clear ninety minutes on a Thursday, so I don't take it lightly.

Let me start somewhere odd.

Some of you will remember the STD booth. The little shop on the corner with the yellow sign, where you'd go to make a call and watch the meter tick. At one point this country had over a million of them. A whole livelihood, on every street, in every town.

They're gone. Not slowly. Just gone.

I wrote about this years ago, long before the words "artificial intelligence" were on every magazine cover. I was asking a simple question back then. When the tool changes, what actually stays? And the honest answer I landed on had nothing to do with the technology. It was the human things. Judgment. Taste. The courage to make a call when nobody can tell you the right answer. The ability to make sense of things when everything is in chaos.

I tell you this so you know where I'm coming from tonight. I am not here to sell you AI. I won't pretend I have it figured out, which is exactly why I asked Moumita to lead this, and why I pulled together a room of people I genuinely respect.

Here is the one thing I'd ask you to hold for the next ninety minutes. AI is not a software decision. It is not something you hand to a vendor or a 25-year-old in your IT team and forget. It is a decision about your business, your people, and the kind of organisation you want to build. That makes it yours. It sits with the person who runs the place. It sits with you.

So that's my only job tonight. To hold the room while Moumita does the real work, and then to connect you to each other after, because half the value in here is sitting in the chairs next to you.

Moumita, it's yours.

---

## The close (6:00 slot, when you connect the room)

> Use this as the thought you leave them holding through high tea.

Here's what I keep coming back to. Every one of you now has access to a team that never sleeps. A hundred AI agents, if you want them.

The question was never whether you'll use AI. You will. The question is whether you'll **own** it or **serve** it.

Hiring that team, pointing it at the right problem, deciding what to build, that's the owner's move. Using it to get through your own to-do list a little faster, that's just becoming a quicker version of the employee in your own business.

You didn't build what you built by being the fastest worker in the room. You built it by deciding what mattered. That doesn't change now. It matters more.

---

## Pocket story for Q&A (if the room leans into "we just need to put AI everywhere")

**The Sarpanch and the doubled wages.** When Adi's father set up his first factory in Chakan, he doubled the workers' wages in year two, certain more money meant a better life. Months later the village Sarpanch came to ask him to *roll it back*. The extra money, dropped on people who weren't ready for it, had brought drinking, gambling, unhappy homes. More of a powerful input is not automatically better. The receiver decides the outcome, not the input. Same with AI poured into a business that hasn't decided what it actually wants from it.

## Delivery notes

- Pause after "Just gone." Let it sit. That beat is the whole hook.
- The line that does the heavy lifting is *"It sits with you."* Slow down there, make eye contact across the room.
- Keep it to 90 seconds. The room came for Moumita's substance, your job is to frame and get out of the way.
- No slides. This is a spoken open, not a deck.
- Don't name Let's Enterprise in the open. The 95/5 rule holds, the invite already says who you are.
